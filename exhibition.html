Automatic Fertilizer Setup

“Feed the Crop, Not the Waste – Smart Farming for a Greener Future.”

Hardware Architecture

ESP32
Central microcontroller coordinating drive, nozzle aiming, and spraying.
4× BO Motors + 2× TB6612FNG
Drive system wheels across custom-built chassis.
Water Pump + L293D
Measured liquid fertilizer spraying assembly.
Metal-Gear Servo
Precision arm aiming nozzle toward crops.
18650 + TP4056 + LM2596
Rechargeable battery pack, safe charging, and voltage regulation.

Opening

[Student 1]

Good morning, everyone! Did you know that the food on our plate begins with the health of our soil? And what if a little robot could look after that soil – carefully, safely, and without wasting a single drop?

The Problem

[Student 1]

Today, fertilizer is mostly spread by hand. It is slow and tiring, and farmers are exposed to harmful chemicals. Worse, too much fertilizer is often applied – wasting money, damaging the soil and washing into rivers and ponds, where it pollutes our water.

Our Solution

[Student 2]

Introducing our Automatic Fertilizer Setup – a smart robotic vehicle controlled by an ESP32 microcontroller. It moves along the crop field and delivers liquid fertilizer in a measured, controlled way, exactly where it is needed.

How Does It Work?

[Student 2 and Student 3]

Every part has a purpose:

  • The Drive System – four BO motors with wheels, controlled by two TB6612FNG motor drivers, move the vehicle smoothly over a custom-built chassis.
  • The Spraying System – a small water pump, run through an L293D motor driver, sprays the liquid fertilizer.
  • The Precision Arm – a metal-gear servo motor positions the nozzle accurately towards the plants.
  • The Brain – the ESP32 coordinates the movement, the aiming and the spraying.

Rechargeable and Reusable

[Student 3]

Our vehicle runs on rechargeable 18650 batteries, charged safely through TP4056 modules, with LM2596 step-down modules supplying the right voltage to each part. No disposable batteries, no waste – just clean, reusable energy.

Why It Matters

[Student 4]

Controlled spraying means less fertilizer wasted, lower costs for farmers, and healthier soil. Cleaner rivers and groundwater. Less chemical contact for the farmer. And more time to focus on what truly matters. A system like this can help even small farmers move toward modern, sustainable agriculture – and support food security for all.

Closing

[All Students]

Our country grows because our farmers grow. With this project, we wish to bring technology to the field and care to the earth. Thank you – because when we take care of the soil, the soil takes care of us!

Less Waste. Healthier Soil. Happier Farmers!

Smart Solar Weather Monitoring System

Sensors & Power

ESP32 + Wi-Fi
Low-cost node reading sensors and sharing live readings over Wi-Fi.
DHT11 & BMP180
Temperature, humidity, and barometric air pressure forecasting.
MQ135 + Rain Sensor + LDR
Air quality alerts, rain detection, and sunlight intensity.
Hall Effect + Neodymium
Rotation-counting wind speed measurement.
16×2 LCD Display
Live local on-station readings readout.
Solar + 2× 18650 + LM2596
Day/night off-grid operation with regulated voltage.

Opening

[Student 1]

Good morning, everyone! Imagine a farmer looking at the sky, wondering: “Will it rain today? Is it safe to harvest?” Today, we bring him an answer – from a small station that runs entirely on sunlight!

The Problem

[Student 1]

Our weather is changing faster than ever. Sudden storms, scorching heat and unpredictable rain affect farmers, schools, villages and cities alike. But big weather stations are costly and far away – so local, accurate information is often missing exactly where it is needed most.

Our Solution

[Student 2]

Meet our Smart Solar Weather Monitoring System – a low-cost, self-powered weather node built around the ESP32 microcontroller. It senses the environment around it, displays the readings live, and shares them over Wi-Fi.

What Does It Measure?

[Student 2 and Student 3]

Our node is a complete mini weather laboratory:

  • Temperature and humidity – using the DHT11 sensor.
  • Air pressure – using the BMP180, the key to forecasting weather changes.
  • Air quality – using the MQ135 gas sensor, to warn us about pollution.
  • Rain – detected instantly by the rain sensor.
  • Sunlight intensity – measured by the LDR.
  • Wind speed – Hall effect sensors detect neodymium magnets on a rotating part, and every rotation is counted to calculate speed.

All readings appear on a 16×2 LCD screen, right in front of you.

Powered by the Sun

[Student 3]

The best part? It never needs a power socket! A solar panel charges two 18650 batteries during the day, and an LM2596 module delivers steady, safe power to the electronics. So our station keeps working – day and night, with zero electricity bills and zero emissions.

Why It Matters

[Student 4]

This node is just one unit – place many of them across fields, villages and schools, and they become a powerful local weather network. Farmers can plan sowing and harvesting. Communities can get early warnings before storms. Students can learn from real data. And everyone can become aware of the air they breathe.

Closing

[All Students]

We believe the future belongs to technology that is clean, affordable and useful to everyone. Our weather station is a small step toward climate awareness and sustainable living. Thank you – and remember: when we understand our weather, we can protect our world!

Small Station. Big Impact. A Smarter, Greener Tomorrow!
